Frequently Asked

FAQ

Do I need to know generals.io to compete?
No prior experience required. The docs cover the game rules in 5 minutes and the starter bot is a working example you can iterate from.
What language can I write my bot in?
Any language that can read and write whitespace-separated integers on stdin/stdout — the protocol is deliberately trivial, and there are reference bots in Python, C++ and Rust. You submit a .zip with a run.sh at its root that starts your bot (plus an optional build.sh to compile once). No Dockerfile — Python 3.12, C/C++ (GCC) and Rust toolchains are baked into the sandbox image (exact versions and the pre-installed Python libraries are listed in Environment); anything else must be shipped inside your zip as a self-contained binary or vendored runtime.
Can I use machine learning?
Yes — train offline and submit the weights inside your zip. There is no network at any point, during the build or the match, so nothing can be downloaded: vendor your dependencies and your checkpoints. Matches are CPU-only (one core per bot), so use CPU builds of your ML framework — CPU torch and jax are already pre-installed (see Environment).
Can we compete as a team?
Yes — teams are welcome. A team competes through one shared account (the email/password option at registration is made for this) and appears as one entry on the leaderboard. The mapping is strict: one account per person or team, and each person can be part of only one account — see fair play.
How many submissions can I make?
You can keep up to 10 stored submissions and make 20 per day, with 5 minutes between them and at most 3 evaluating at once. Delete a stored one to free a slot. Your active agent — the one that represents you on the leaderboard — is always your most recent submission that passed its checks, so improving your bot is just submitting again.
How are matches scheduled?
Continuously, in two layers. Every submission first plays a quick qualification check — 3 games against the expander baseline (the gate) and 3 against hunter (informational) — so you get feedback within minutes. To qualify, your bot must run without crashing and win a majority of its expander games (2 of 3); the hunter record is shown on your submissions page but never gates. A bot that fails stays off the leaderboard until you upload one that passes — your previously qualified bot, if you have one, keeps its place. A passing bot is immediately placed: it plays a spread of opponents around its estimated strength and joins the leaderboard with a provisional Elo (marked ?). Then a full round robin every 6 hours fills in every pairing not yet played — each pairing runs on 3 maps from both sides, so nobody is advantaged by their starting corner. Ranked play begins on August 1.
Do I get the prize if I win?
If you're on the eligible-for-prize list (i.e. not an Equilibre/ÚFAL employee) and finish in a prize position, yes — we contact you by email within a few days of the deadline and pay within 30 days.
Is my code public?
Only if you publish it yourself. Your uploaded zip is stored privately and is never served from the site — all anyone else sees is your bot's name, its rank, and the replays of its matches.
What happens after the competition ends?
The leaderboard freezes at the deadline. Top results are announced on stream. Replays remain available indefinitely. Future seasons may follow — we'll email everyone on the list.
I found a bug / I have an idea — where do I report it?
Open an issue on the GitHub repo, or ping us in Discord. PRs welcome.
