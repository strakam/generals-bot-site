About the competition

01 — What this is

The Generals Competition is an open contest for generals.io bots. Build a program that plays the game well, submit it, and we run it against everyone else's submissions continuously. Top-ranked bots take the prize pool.

It's organized by Equilibre Technologies in collaboration with ÚFAL (the Institute of Formal and Applied Linguistics at Charles University). Open to anyone — students, hobbyists, researchers.

02 — The game

Generals.io is a fast-paced strategy game played on a rectangular grid: expand your territory, grow your army, capture castles, and take the opponent's general before they take yours. This competition runs a programmatic version with the same rules, played by bots rather than humans.

The complete ruleset — the board, turns and moves, army growth, combat, the Round 1 parameters, and match constraints — lives on the Rules page.

Strongly recommended: spend 10 minutes playing a few rounds or watching replays on generals.io yourself before you start writing a bot. Reading the rules tells you what's legal; playing tells you why expansion tempo and castle timing actually matter.

03 — Format

One competition, one ruleset, one continuous leaderboard — with two prize checkpoints (see the landing page for amounts).

  • Sprint — the first checkpoint, August 8 (one week in). The top of the leaderboard advances to a Sprint tournament — a thorough head-to-head evaluation that decides the top 3 and their prizes. The race keeps going.
  • Marathon — the finish, end of August. The top of the board advances to the final tournament that decides the champion and the Marathon prizes.

Bots play continuously against other submissions throughout. A new submission is placed against opponents of similar skill within minutes, and a round robin every four hours fills in every remaining pairing. Rankings are recomputed by Elo after every batch — a rating stays marked provisional (?) until the bot has played most of the field. Standings at each checkpoint determine that checkpoint's payout.

04 — Anti-cheat & fair play

We have zero tolerance for cheating. Every submission runs in an isolated sandbox and is scanned for malicious intent, and any attempt to cheat or sabotage the competition results in disqualification and a permanent ban.

The bar is independence: every submission must represent its account's own work. Teams are welcome — a team competes through one shared account — but the mapping is strict: one account per person or team, each person on at most one account, and no sharing code, weights, or strategies between accounts. The sandbox redacts identifying info — attempting to deanonymize, read, or interfere with other bots is forbidden. We review match logs and investigate anomalous patterns, like suspiciously similar play across accounts.

05 — Conduct

Be respectful in Discord and elsewhere. Harassment, slurs, and bad-faith behavior get you removed. These rules may change as the competition runs; any update is announced at least 7 days before it takes effect.
